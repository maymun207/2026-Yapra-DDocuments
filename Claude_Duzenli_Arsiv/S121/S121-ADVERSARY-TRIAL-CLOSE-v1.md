## 1 · RUN 3 ARGUED AGAINST ITSELF, AND IT IS RIGHT

The target was the document proposing the seat the candidate would occupy. It declared the
conflict in its second paragraph and then made the case for **not** building the seat — which is
the behaviour the sealed bake-off rubric declares its own conflict in order to model.

**Its central recommendation, quoted verbatim from `S121-ADVERSARY-VERDICT-CODEX-3.md` §4:**

> *"The better move is narrower: require adversarial review as a disposable lens for
> decision-moving documents, with repo access and a predeclared checklist. Do not institutionalize
> it as a seat. … Archive adversary outputs as evidence, not as governance."*

**This seat accepts it, and the measurement supports it over the incumbent's own proposal.** Across
three runs the value tracked **the channel and the brief**, never a standing role: run 1 had the
role and no repository and produced nothing factual; run 2 had the same role plus a repository and
a scoped brief and produced two load-bearing hits. **Nothing measured in this trial required a
seat.** A seat adds standing authority, a reconciliation burden, and another artefact class to
govern — the exact machinery that consumed S120 — and buys nothing the three runs demonstrated.

**The first one inverts the meaning and then refutes a claim nobody made.** The target attributes
those surfaces to the **Architect**; the fabrication attributes them to **Codex/OpenAI**, and §2.A's
entire refutation — *"the document treats live channel inventory as measured"* — is built on that

The line numbers are also wrong or unverifiable: the target is **131 lines**; the citations
("81-87", "91-95", "116-120") are close enough to look checkable and do not resolve to the quoted
text.

**Why this is HIGH rather than pedantry.** An adversary's entire product is fidelity to a text it
alone read. A paraphrase wearing quotation marks and a line number is indistinguishable from a
measurement until someone greps for it — and S120's own lesson is that **a wrong finding that
reaches the archive is worse than none, because the next session trusts the archive instead of
re-measuring.** This defect is the exact failure mode adversarial review exists to prevent, in the
instrument built to prevent it.

**It does not rescue the incumbent.** Run 3's §3 and §4 — the falsifier critique and the
seat-versus-lens argument — depend on no quotation at all, and both are upheld. Dismissing the
argument because of the quotes would be the defensive move this seat is supposed to be immune to.

## 4 · THE RULING

**The Architect adversary is a LENS, not a SEAT.** Nothing is institutionalised, no channel is
built, no standing authority is created.

**The standing rule, effective now:**

> **Any document that would change what gets built next goes through one adversarial review before
> it is acted on.** The reviewer gets the public repository and a brief scoped to decision-changing
> claims. Its output is archived **as evidence, never as governance** — it rules nothing, it
> refutes.

**Two conditions the trial proved are load-bearing and are therefore part of the rule:**

1. **Repository attached.** Run 1 without it produced zero factual findings. A file-only reviewer
   refutes prose and cannot reach the world.
2. **Every quoted string is verifiable, and the incumbent greps it before acting.** Run 3 proves an
   unverified quotation survives all the way to a Direct Answer. **No claim from an adversary
   enters an artefact until this seat has confirmed it against the primary source** — which is
   already the law (S102, derivative-never-source); run 3 is its proof.

**The owner's S120 ruling is untouched.** Codex/OpenAI remains the adversary and Fable5 remains the
named drafter. What changes is that neither becomes a *seat* until something measured requires one,
and nothing measured here does. **The drafter channel remains UNMEASURED and unbuilt, and no work
is proposed on it.**
