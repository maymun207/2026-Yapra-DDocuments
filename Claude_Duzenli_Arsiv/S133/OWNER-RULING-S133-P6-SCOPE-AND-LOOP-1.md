## WHAT IT DECIDES, AND IT IS TWO THINGS

**FIRST HALF — the freeze COVERS the adversary mechanism.** The Architect's proposed termination rule was a threshold written into the mechanism: *all amendments in a round being self-repairs AND the count not falling → RELEASE the last clean version.* That is a governance change and it is NOT INSTALLED. It does not go in quietly, it does not go in as a card convention, and it does not go in as "the Architect's practice". It waits for P-6 to close. The observation window means what it says: ordinary lane work runs, the mechanism waits.

**SECOND HALF — and this is the owner's sentence, not a version of the Architect's.** *An infinite loop is stopped wherever it is seen.* The Architect proposed a THRESHOLD; the owner replaced it with a JUDGEMENT STANDARD. The difference is the whole point and is recorded as his contribution:

- A threshold is a mechanism: it fires by itself, on a number, whether or not anyone is looking. That is what the freeze forbids.
- A standard is an obligation on the actor: the Architect must LOOK, and having seen a loop, must STOP it. No new mechanism is created; an existing authority is directed. The freeze does not touch it.

The two halves are consistent, and the Architect had not seen that they could be. Recorded so a later window does not re-derive the threshold and mistake it for this ruling.

## WHAT STOPPING MEANS, STATED BEFORE IT HAPPENS

So the word carries no ambiguity when it is used: to STOP a line is to post the `RELEASE-<name>` row on the last version whose defects were defects in the WORK, and to carry the remaining amendment as an open finding against the PRODUCED artefact rather than against the card — where CI, the producer's own FALSIFIER and the landing review test it. It is not abandonment and it is not "good enough": the finding survives by name, it is simply moved to the surface that can measure it. SOTA-1 is untouched, because no criterion is retired and nothing is deferred for convenience.

**And the judgement stays with the Architect and visible to the owner.** If the next verdict on a line proposes amendments that are ALL review-repairs by the scout's own classification, and the count does not fall, the Architect stops that line, posts the RELEASE, and tells the owner IN ONE LINE at the moment it happens — announced, not asked, because the owner has ruled; and announced rather than silent, so he can countermand a stop he thinks premature before the producer has spent a run on it.

## WHAT THIS RULING DOES NOT DO

It does not authorise stopping a line because it is slow, expensive, or tiresome. A loop is a line that consumes rounds without reducing defects in the WORK. Twenty adversary verdicts with zero RELEASE rows is a fact about this session and is NOT by itself a loop — every one of those verdicts named a defect that was really there, and several were the Architect's own.
